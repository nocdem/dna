Privacy Policy

Nodus Connect — Post-Quantum Encrypted Communication App. Current Android, Linux, and Windows releases retain the DNA Connect name.

1. Overview

Nodus Connect is a decentralized, peer-to-peer messaging application built on post-quantum cryptography. This privacy policy explains what data the application accesses, how it is used, and your rights as a user.

Nodus Connect does not collect, store, or transmit any personal data to centralized servers. As with any internet connection, the network nodes your device connects to can see your IP address and connection timing. All communication is end-to-end encrypted using NIST-approved post-quantum algorithms (Kyber1024 for key encapsulation, Dilithium5 for signatures).

2. Data We Do NOT Collect

Nodus Connect is designed with privacy as its core principle. We do not collect:

  • Personal information (name, email, phone number)
  • Message content — all messages are end-to-end encrypted and only readable by the intended recipient
  • Contact lists or address books from your device
  • Location data
  • Usage analytics or telemetry
  • Advertising identifiers
  • Device fingerprints for tracking purposes
  • Browsing history or app usage patterns

One exception — opt-in debug logs. The app offers a feature at Settings → Data & Storage → Send Debug Log to Developer that lets you voluntarily send your application log to the developer for troubleshooting. When you use this feature: the log is scrubbed of mnemonics, private keys, and credential-like values; then hybrid-encrypted (Kyber1024 + AES-256-GCM) to the developer's public key; and delivered via the DHT. No other party (including DHT nodes) can read the log. This feature is entirely optional and only runs when you explicitly tap the button. Logs expire after 1 hour if not delivered.

3. Data Stored Locally on Your Device

The following data is stored exclusively on your device and never transmitted to us:

Cryptographic Material

Dilithium5 signing key pair
Purpose: Identity verification and message signing
Storage: Local file (identity.dsa), derived from recovery phrase. On Android, wrapped by Android Keystore TEE (hardware-backed AES-256-GCM) — the wrapping key never leaves secure hardware.
Kyber1024 encryption key pair
Purpose: Post-quantum key encapsulation
Storage: Local file (identity.kem), derived from recovery phrase. On Android, wrapped by Android Keystore TEE (hardware-backed AES-256-GCM) — the wrapping key never leaves secure hardware.
Recovery phrase (24 words)
Purpose: Account backup and recovery
Storage: Encrypted with Kyber1024 public key (mnemonic.enc). Never stored as plain text.
DHT node identity
Purpose: Authentication on the decentralized network
Storage: Cached locally (dht_identity.bin), deterministically derived from recovery phrase

Application Data

Contact list
Purpose: Managing trusted contacts, block list
Storage: Local SQLite database (contacts.db)
Message history
Purpose: Conversation backup and display
Storage: Local SQLite database (messages.db)
Profile cache
Purpose: Caching display names, avatars, and public keys of contacts
Storage: Local SQLite databases (profiles.db, keyserver_cache.db)
Wall post cache
Purpose: Offline access to public posts, comments, and likes
Storage: Local SQLite database (wall_cache.db)
Group & channel data
Purpose: Group memberships, encrypted group keys, channel subscriptions (channels are currently disabled)
Storage: Local SQLite databases (groups.db, channel_subscriptions.db)
Wallet balance & transaction cache
Purpose: Displaying wallet balances and transaction history
Storage: Local SQLite database (wallet_cache.db). Private keys are never stored — derived on-demand from recovery phrase.
Wallet address book
Purpose: Saved recipient addresses with labels
Storage: Local SQLite database (addressbook.db)
App lock PIN
Purpose: Optional app lock authentication
Storage: PBKDF2-SHA256 hash with random salt, stored in Android Keystore (iOS Keychain on the planned iOS version) (encrypted by OS)
App preferences
Purpose: Settings such as biometric lock, notification preferences
Storage: OS-managed local storage (SharedPreferences)

Local SQLite databases are encrypted at rest using SQLCipher (AES-256-CBC with per-page HMAC-SHA512 integrity). Each database's encryption key is derived from your identity material using PBKDF2 and stored in the OS keystore — Android Keystore (iOS Keychain on the planned iOS version). If your device is lost or stolen, database contents cannot be read even with physical access. Sensitive cryptographic material (recovery phrase, PIN) uses additional dedicated encryption as described above.

4. Decentralized Network (DHT)

Nodus Connect uses a decentralized hash table (DHT) network called Nodus for message delivery and profile discovery. Data published to this network falls into two categories:

Public Data (readable by DHT nodes and other users)

  • Public profile: Your public cryptographic keys, registered display name, bio, avatar, location, website, social links, and wallet addresses. This data is intentionally public to allow others to find and communicate with you.
  • Wall posts, comments, and likes: Public posts you choose to publish, along with any comments or likes, are visible to anyone on the network. Posts are signed but not encrypted.
  • Public channels are currently disabled and will be re-enabled in a future release. Channel posts and metadata: Public channel content including channel name, description, and posts are readable by DHT nodes.

Encrypted Data (not readable by DHT nodes)

  • Direct messages: Encrypted with Kyber1024 key encapsulation and AES-256-GCM. Only the intended recipient can decrypt them.
  • Group messages: Encrypted with a Group Encryption Key (GEK), distributed to group members via Kyber1024 encapsulation.
  • Contact lists and address books: Self-encrypted with your own Kyber1024 public key.
  • Message backups: Self-encrypted with your own Kyber1024 public key.
  • Group membership lists: Self-encrypted with your own Kyber1024 public key.
  • Media attachments (images, videos, voice messages): Encrypted with Kyber1024 key encapsulation + AES-256-GCM. Files are chunked and stored on the Nodus media storage layer; only the intended recipient (or group members) can decrypt them.

No centralized server stores or has access to your private keys. DHT nodes can read public profile information and wall posts, but cannot read your private messages, contact lists, or group memberships. As with any internet connection, the network nodes your device connects to can see your IP address and connection timing.

6. Third-Party Services

Nodus Connect does not integrate any third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking services. Specifically:

  • No Google Analytics or Firebase Analytics
  • No Facebook SDK
  • No advertising networks
  • No crash reporting services that transmit data externally

The application connects to the following third-party services for wallet functionality:

  • Blockchain RPC endpoints (Cellframe, Ethereum, BSC, Solana, TRON) for wallet balance queries and transaction broadcasting. These connections transmit only your public wallet address — never private keys.
  • BitcoinTry API (api.bitcointry.com) for real-time cryptocurrency price data. No personal data or wallet addresses are sent — only token pair identifiers (e.g., ETH_USDT).

7. Encryption & Security

All cryptographic operations use NIST-approved post-quantum algorithms:

  • Key Encapsulation: ML-KEM (Kyber1024) — NIST Category 5
  • Digital Signatures: ML-DSA (Dilithium5) — NIST Category 5
  • Symmetric Encryption: AES-256-GCM for message content
  • Hashing: SHA3-512 for identity fingerprints

These algorithms are designed to resist attacks from both classical and quantum computers. Your private keys never leave your device.

8. Data Retention & Deletion

  • All data is stored on your device. Uninstalling the app deletes all local data.
  • Public profile data is periodically refreshed on the network by your device to maintain availability.
  • Wall posts have a 30-day TTL on the network and expire automatically.
  • You can delete your account at any time by uninstalling the app. There is no server-side account to delete.

9. Children's Privacy

Nodus Connect does not knowingly collect any information from children under the age of 13. The application does not require any personal information to create an account — only a locally-generated cryptographic key pair.
